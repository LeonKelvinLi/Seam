Impossible cuts from internet moments

The transition is the product demo. This brief finds the internet moments whose motion actually earns an AI transition, pairs them by visual continuity, writes the generative prompts, and marks the rights reality: every public celebrity clip below is a creative reference, not campaign footage. The recreation route for each pair is included.

How clips were judged

Every candidate was scored 1–10 on Recognition, Hook, Humor, Motion, Transition potential, Visual quality and Remix potential. The keep rule is the brief's: roughly the strongest fifth survives, and a lesser-known clip with incredible transition potential beats a famous face with none. Timestamps are exact where the moment is the whole short; for moments inside long streams the row says confirm, because no connected tool could scrub those streams this week.

Three footage tests decide the pairing: what is the dominant motion in the last half second, what object or shape is in frame, and what does the end frame leave behind (a colour, a silhouette, a hole in the frame). The next clip is chosen to continue exactly that.

Final selection

Ad structure and formats

Eight to eighteen seconds. Start on the moment, never on a logo. Each transition must be more impressive than the last, and the product reveal only arrives after curiosity has been established.

0.0–1.0 s
Recognisable moment, mid-action. No intro.
1–4 s
First AI transition. Motivated by the motion already on screen.
4–8 s
Second transition, noticeably bigger (environment or physics).
8–12 s
Third transition, the escalation (scale or semantic morph).
12–15 s
Reveal: one line of copy, three seconds of the interface, end card.

Sound

The original reaction audio is the joke; keep it wherever it lands, and let the transition sound answer it. A whoosh is only justified when something physically crosses the lens, a riser only when the camera is accelerating toward something, an impact only when matter changes state (chair → coaster harness, bottle → rocket). Ambience carries through the seam: room tone under the first frames of the new world, the new world's air fading in under the last frames of the old one. Sync the transformation to a speech accent or beat, and protect the comedic pause before the punchline. Per-combo notes are on each card.

Rights and the recreation plan

Not cleared (all creator clips above)

  • A publicly viewable clip is not a licence. Every row marked reference is study material only.
  • Two rows carry a second layer: World Cup reactions have match broadcast on screen (FIFA and broadcaster rights); the WrestleMania splash is WWE property; the KSI song is a label master.
  • Prime bottles carry a trademark; recreate with unbranded bottles.

Three routes to a usable Clip A

  • Creator permission. Speed, Kai, AMP, Sidemen and Adin all sell licensing through management; the Lamborghini jump and the Sidemen road trip are the creators' own uploads, the cleanest asks.
  • Recreate the beat. A chair, a door, a ring light and a hired performer reproduce every kept moment for a few hundred dollars. The grammar is what we are borrowing, not the face.
  • Generate a look-alike. A generic performer with no real likeness; the hero below was made this way today.
