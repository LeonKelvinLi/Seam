# Current design scorecard against this brief

Scope is the homepage, not the product engine. Evidence links refer to
01-evidence.md. Scores apply the skill's conservative rubric; this is a design
assessment, not a usability study. More motion is an explicit user preference.

1. Innovative — 2/3. Evidence: Copy and actual media. Real scroll seeking improves
   the familiar layered-card pattern; no five-product originality study supports 3.
2. Useful — 3/3. Evidence: Structure. A direct create action and sample route make
   the primary next step available without a detour.
3. Aesthetic — 1/3. Evidence: Visual evidence. A full-screen white process section
   is one jarring violation of the requested dark media environment.
4. Understandable — 1/3. Evidence: Copy and actual media. Two unclear affordances
   (illustrative playhead and unclickable Watch the result text) accompany jargon.
5. Unobtrusive — 1/3. Evidence: Structure / Weight. Multiple independent decorations
   compete with source media; measured hero media starts low in the first viewport.
6. Honest — 1/3. Evidence: Copy and actual media. Two impossible claims and an
   unsynchronized editor-like playhead weaken literal correspondence; no deception
   in pricing or dark patterns was found.
7. Long-lasting — 2/3. Evidence: Visual evidence / Structure. Native controls and
   stable type survive; floating glass/gradient decoration is a trend marker.
8. Thorough — 2/3. Evidence: Visual evidence. Media/focus/error handling exists,
   but scrub state announcement and gradient-caption contrast remain rough.
9. Environmentally friendly — 0/3. Evidence: Weight and friction. Skill rubric
   assigns 0 for autoplay video despite gating and modest estimated JS transfer.
   This is not a recommendation to remove the motion requested by the user.
10. As little design as possible — 1/3. Evidence: Structure. Removable orbit rings,
    empty bridge planes, floating tag and repeated explanatory treatments remain.

Total: 14/30.
